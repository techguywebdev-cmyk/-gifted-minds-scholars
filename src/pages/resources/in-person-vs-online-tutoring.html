In-person vs. online tutoring for elementary students

Both formats can work well. In-person tutoring is usually best for younger children (Pre-K to 2nd grade), hands-on learners and children who struggle to focus on a screen. Online tutoring is ideal when convenience and scheduling matter most, or when you live farther away, and it works especially well for older elementary students who are comfortable with a computer.

Key takeaways

  • Younger and hands-on learners often do best in person.
  • Online saves travel time and widens your scheduling options.
  • The tutor’s experience and connection with your child matter more than the format.
  • You can start with one format and switch if it isn’t working.

When is in-person tutoring the better choice?

Young children learn through touch, movement and close interaction. In-person tutoring makes it easy to use physical materials, correct pencil grip and letter formation, and read a child’s body language in the moment. It’s also a strong choice for children who are easily distracted on a screen and for many children with ADHD or other learning differences who benefit from multisensory, hands-on strategies.

When is online tutoring the better choice?

Online tutoring removes travel time, which can make after-school schedules much easier. It also lets families work with a tutor they trust even if they don’t live nearby. Older elementary students who can navigate a device and stay engaged often do very well online, especially for reading comprehension, writing and math practice.

How do I make online tutoring work for a young child?

  • Set up a quiet spot with a stable internet connection and good lighting.
  • Use a laptop or tablet rather than a phone so your child can see clearly.
  • Keep pencils, paper and any materials the tutor suggests within reach.
  • Stay nearby for the first few sessions, especially for Pre-K–1st grade.

What matters more than the format?

The biggest factor is the tutor: their experience with your child’s age group, their patience, how well they connect with your child, and how clearly they communicate with you about progress. A great tutor will help you decide which format suits your child — and adjust if needed.
